# AudioVisualCaps

**Listening without Looking: Modality Bias in Audio-Visual Captioning**
Yuchi Ishikawa, Toranosuke Manabe, Tatsuya Komatsu, Yoshimitsu Aoki
*IEEE International Conference on Image Processing (ICIP) 2026*

[[IEEE Xplore](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICIP61757.2026.11630322)] [[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24024)]

This repository provides **AudioVisualCaps**, an extension of [AudioCaps](https://github.com/cdjkim/audiocaps) with textual annotations that jointly describe the audio and visual streams of each clip. The dataset is introduced in our ICIP 2026 paper to evaluate how balanced audio-visual captioning models are in their use of both modalities.

## Overview

Audio-visual captioning aims to generate holistic scene descriptions by jointly modeling sound and vision. While recent methods have improved performance through sophisticated modality fusion, it remains unclear to what extent the two modalities are complementary in current audio-visual captioning models, and how robust these models are when one modality is degraded.

We address these questions by conducting systematic modality robustness tests on [LAVCap](https://github.com/Hyeongkeun/LAVCap), a state-of-the-art audio-visual captioning model, in which we selectively suppress or corrupt the audio or visual streams to quantify sensitivity and complementarity. The analysis reveals a pronounced bias toward the audio stream in LAVCap. To evaluate how balanced audio-visual captioning models are, we augment AudioCaps with captions that describe both the audio and visual content, yielding the **AudioVisualCaps** dataset. LAVCap trained on AudioVisualCaps exhibits less modality bias than when trained on AudioCaps.

## Contributions

- **Modality robustness tests.** We propose a systematic evaluation protocol that selectively suppresses or corrupts the audio or visual stream, quantifying how sensitive an audio-visual captioning model is to each modality and how complementary the two modalities are.
- **Evidence of audio bias.** Applying the tests to LAVCap reveals a pronounced bias toward the audio stream: captions are largely driven by sound, and the visual stream contributes little.
- **AudioVisualCaps dataset.** We extend AudioCaps with frame-level visual captions and audio-visual merged captions that jointly describe what is heard and what is seen, providing a benchmark for balanced audio-visual captioning.
- **Baselines and analysis.** We report LAVCap baseline results on AudioVisualCaps and show that training on AudioVisualCaps reduces modality bias compared with training on AudioCaps.

## Dataset

### Files

| File | Clips | Columns |
|---|---|---|
| `data/audiovisualcaps_train.parquet` | 49,838 | `id`, `audio_captions`, `visual_captions` |
| `data/audiovisualcaps_val.parquet` | 495 | `id`, `audio_captions`, `visual_captions` |
| `data/audiovisualcaps_test.parquet` | 975 | `id`, `audio_captions`, `visual_captions`, `merged_captions` |

### Columns

| Column | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | string | Clip identifier in the form `{youtube_id}_{start_time}`, e.g. `-W1-HZroSuw_30`. It matches the video / audio file names of AudioCaps. |
| `audio_captions` | list[string] | Human-written audio captions from the official AudioCaps annotations (1 caption per clip for train, 5 for val and test). |
| `visual_captions` | list[string] | Frame-level visual captions generated by [BLIP-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12597). Three frames are sampled per clip, yielding 3 captions. |
| `merged_captions` | list[string] | Audio-visual captions generated by gpt-oss-20b, integrating one audio caption with the visual captions of the clip (test only, 5 per clip, aligned index-wise with `audio_captions`). |

### Example

```python
import pandas as pd

test = pd.read_parquet("data/audiovisualcaps_test.parquet")
row = test.iloc[0]

print(row["id"])
# --0w1YA1Hm4_30

print(row["audio_captions"][0])
# A vehicle driving as a man and woman are talking and laughing

print(list(row["visual_captions"]))
# ['a red bus driving down a road at night',
#  'a car driving down the road at night',
#  'a red truck driving down a road at night']

print(row["merged_captions"][0])
# A red bus is driving at night while a man and woman talk and laugh.
```

### How the merged captions were created

Each audio caption is combined with the three visual captions of the same clip by [gpt-oss-20b](https://huggingface.co/openai/gpt-oss-20b), which is instructed to:

1. include at least one visual element that is difficult to infer from audio alone (e.g. type of vehicle, number of people, color, location);
2. explicitly mention at least one sound event from the audio caption;
3. prefer visual evidence for objects, actions, places and species, and audio evidence for the presence or absence of sound events when the two conflict;
4. avoid proper names and information derived from on-screen text;
5. output a single English sentence of at most 25 words.

### Audio and video data

This repository contains **annotations only**. The original audio and video clips are YouTube segments and must be obtained by following the instructions in the official AudioCaps repository:

https://github.com/cdjkim/audiocaps

The `id` column corresponds to `{youtube_id}_{start_time}` in the official `train.csv`, `val.csv` and `test.csv`.
